# When to add a framework

Start with the claim a reader could act on. Ask three different questions: What teacher practice or growth measure does the local system recognize? What thinking must the student do? What research supports the named intervention? Each question has a different unit of analysis. A student work sample can answer a classroom question without answering any of these.

## Teacher Incentive Allotment (TIA)

Include a TIA connection when observation or longitudinal growth is germane and the example can be tied to the district's approved local designation system. Verify the relevant eligible teaching assignment and local rubric or growth measure before making a specific alignment. Districts can differ in the assignments, measures, and protocols they use.

- **Teacher observation:** Identify a specific teacher move, the corresponding local rubric indicator, and observable evidence. For example, capture how the teacher elicits competing predictions, then asks both students to revise using graph evidence. If local documentation is available, cite the indicator verbatim or identify it accurately.
- **Student growth:** Identify the locally approved measure, comparable baseline and follow-up evidence, scoring procedure, and time window. If the district uses an approved student learning objective, follow its body-of-evidence protocol.
- **Omit** for an ordinary activity, a single exit slip, an unknown local system, or a product whose only connection is that teachers can save student work. If TIA is explicitly requested but the local plan is missing, provide a conditional planning note outside the image and identify the missing local information. Reserve designation, rating, and qualifying-growth claims for the authorized local process.

Source: [Texas TIA system components](https://tiatexas.org/for-districts/components-of-a-system/).

## Webb’s Depth of Knowledge (DOK)

Include a level when describing the task's required reasoning helps the teacher choose, adapt, or assess it. Read the complete prompt, stimulus, scaffolds, and expected response. Classify the minimum cognitive demand required for a successful answer in this subject. Verbs, difficulty for a learner, response length, and elapsed time alone cannot establish a level.

| Level | Required thinking | Graphing example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Recall and reproduction | Retrieve a fact or carry out a familiar procedure. | Read a labeled intercept from a graph. |
| 2 · Skills and concepts | Use a relationship, organize information, or compare representations. | Compare two lines and describe how their different slopes appear on the graph. |
| 3 · Strategic thinking | Decide how to reason through a nonroutine problem; justify a conclusion using evidence. | Resolve competing models for a pattern. Select a test case that distinguishes them and defend the choice. |
| 4 · Extended thinking | Integrate and synthesize across a sustained investigation, evaluating and revising an approach. | Design a campus temperature study across sites and days, revise the sampling plan, and evaluate how well the model generalizes. |

Write “DOK 3 for the model-selection task: students design a distinguishing test and justify it.” If the teacher supplies the method and students merely read the result, reconsider the level. Omit speculative labels when the source gives only a topic or feature. Do not assign one level to a whole app or aim for all four levels. [WebbAlign on cognitive complexity](https://www.webbalign.org/difficulty-and-complexity).

## ESSA Tiers of Evidence

Include a tier when a reader needs to judge the research behind a defined intervention, strategy, or activity. Verify the study or authoritative review, intervention version, measured outcome, study quality, setting and population, and relevance to this implementation. Verify the applicable statutory or program criteria; study design alone is insufficient. Use current primary sources and give the actual study or review link alongside the claim.

| Tier | Evidence to examine |
|---|---|
| 1 · Strong | At least one well-designed, well-implemented experimental study with a statistically significant favorable effect. Check the additional applicable WWC and local relevance criteria. |
| 2 · Moderate | At least one qualifying quasi-experimental study or other study accepted at this level, with a statistically significant favorable effect. Check baseline comparability where appropriate and applicable relevance criteria. |
| 3 · Promising | At least one well-designed, well-implemented correlational study with controls for selection bias and a statistically significant favorable effect. |
| 4 · Demonstrates a rationale | A rationale grounded in high-quality research or positive evaluation explaining why the defined activity should improve a specified outcome, plus ongoing efforts to examine its effects. Identify who is examining which outcome and how. A proposed evaluation alone does not establish the tier. |

Keep evidence for the teaching practice attached to that practice. A study of retrieval practice, a Hattie effect size, curriculum alignment, or an improvement on a class quiz does not establish a product's tier. A WWC practice-guide recommendation has its own rating system. When evidence or applicability cannot be verified, omit the tier; if asked, state what evidence is missing. For a prospective pilot, label the rationale and evaluation plan as proposed. If a grant-specific evidence threshold matters, check it separately.

Sources: [WWC guide to ESSA evidence tiers](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/essa) and [20 USC §7801(21)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t20/s7801/).

## Placement examples

- **Routine bellringer:** Give the recall prompt and the misconception to look for. Omit all three lenses unless the audience has a concrete reason to see one.
- **Lesson-design workshop:** Add a single DOK note comparing reading a data point with selecting evidence to resolve competing explanations. Retain the five program rows.
- **Local coaching meeting:** Add a TIA note only for the documented local indicator or measure. Keep any evaluator-facing specifics in the caption.
- **Research briefing:** Put the intervention, verified tier, study citation, outcome, and population fit in a source note. If the design cannot carry the detail legibly, use a short accompanying table.
