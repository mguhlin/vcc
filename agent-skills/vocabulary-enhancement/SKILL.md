# Vocabulary Enhancement

## Purpose

Help educators teach vocabulary through meaningful contexts, repeated retrieval, creative application, and student explanation.

Create classroom-ready activities adapted to grade level, language proficiency, and instructional goals.

## When to Activate

Use when the user:
- Provides a vocabulary list
- Requests vocabulary activities or games
- Wants word poetry, comics, or storyboards
- Needs differentiated word practice
- Requests multilingual vocabulary supports
- Wants formative assessment or retrieval practice
- Needs creative vocabulary lessons

## Inputs

Use:
- Target words
- Definitions or source passage
- Grade level
- Subject area
- Language proficiency, when provided
- Time available
- Preferred activity format

If definitions are absent, provide accurate definitions and identify meanings that require context.

Do not invent word origins or uncertain meanings.

## Instructional Workflow

1. Identify target words and intended meanings.
2. Select activities appropriate to the learners.
3. Introduce words in meaningful contexts.
4. Model accurate word usage.
5. Require students to explain or apply meanings.
6. Include retrieval practice.
7. Provide a quick formative assessment.

## Grade-Band Activities

### Grades K–2

Picture Poems:
Create short, understandable rhymes using target words.

Movement Vocabulary:
Use gestures or actions to demonstrate meanings.

Word Family Trees:
Explore related forms and familiar word patterns.

Include drawings, oral explanations, and picture supports.

### Grades 3–5

Haiku Challenge:
Create a 5-7-5 syllable poem using a target word meaningfully.

Comic Strip Vocabulary:
Design four panels showing a word in context.

Word Detective:
Find and explain vocabulary in a passage.

### Grades 6–8

Vocabulary Slam:
Create short spoken-word performances using target terms accurately.

Word Origins:
Examine meaningful roots and word histories when verified.

Synonym Gradients:
Arrange related words by intensity or nuance.

Context Clue Mysteries:
Write short mysteries requiring vocabulary interpretation.

### Grades 9–12

Spoken Word:
Use academic vocabulary in a purposeful performance.

Academic Register:
Rewrite informal text in an appropriate academic style.

Morphology:
Analyze prefixes, roots, and suffixes.

Cross-Curricular Transfer:
Apply target vocabulary to a new subject or situation.

## Vocabulary Poetry Slam

When requested, use:

### Round 1: Definition
Explain the word in accessible language.

### Round 2: Context
Use the word accurately in three different contexts.

### Round 3: Connections
Connect the word to related meanings, synonyms, or contrasts.

### Round 4: Personal Application
Invite the learner to create an original verse.

Optional rubric:
- Accurate word usage: 10 points
- Meaningful context: 10 points
- Creative expression: 10 points

Prioritize accurate meaning over rhyme.

## Stop-Motion Vocabulary

Provide a twelve-frame storyboard:

Frames 1–3:
Introduce the word.

Frames 4–6:
Show its meaning visually.

Frames 7–9:
Demonstrate the word in context.

Frames 10–12:
Show a new application or memorable conclusion.

Include:
- Frame descriptions
- Materials
- Narration or caption suggestions
- Student reflection question

A storyboard is a plan, not a rendered animation.

## Differentiation

Offer multiple ways to engage with words:
- Visual representations
- Oral explanations
- Written examples
- Movement activities
- Word analysis
- Collaborative discussion

Do not assign students to fixed "learning styles."

For multilingual learners:
- Allow home-language connections.
- Offer bilingual examples when accurate.
- Explain academic and conversational meanings.
- Provide sentence stems and cognates when appropriate.

For students needing additional support:
- Reduce the number of words.
- Use high-quality examples and nonexamples.
- Increase supported practice.
- Provide accessible formats.

## Assessment

Include one or more:
- Retrieval exit ticket
- Context sentence
- Word-definition match
- Explain-the-word prompt
- Peer explanation
- Short transfer task

Avoid assessing recognition alone when deeper understanding is expected.

## Safeguards

- Use age-appropriate, inclusive language.
- Keep word meanings accurate.
- Avoid invented etymologies.
- Make every activity accessible without special technology.
- Distinguish sample products from student work.
- Do not claim to have generated audio, images, or videos unless those assets were created.
- Do not request personal student data.
